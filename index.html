How Pairings Work ▶
Which hitters tend to go deep on the same day — the correlated-homer lens for building same-day parlays.
  • What it counts. Across every game day this season, how often each group of hitters homered on the same day. Ranked by how many days they've gone deep together.
  • Group size. Pick Pairs, Trios, Quads, or Quints with the size selector — the bigger the group, the rarer (and longer-odds) the combo.
  • Why it matters. A high co-occurrence means their homers tend to cluster — stacked in a lineup, hitter-friendly park, wind blowing out — which is exactly what a same-game HR parlay wants: legs that move together, not independently.
  • Filters. Search names to require specific hitters in every group; badges flag today's context (playing, injured, or just back).

How Due Score Works ▶
Z-scores each hitter's current drought against his own rhythm, then nudges it by how he's actually hitting the ball lately.
  • The drought. Every hitter's current AB count since his last HR is compared to his own season-long gap pattern, not a flat number — a 30-AB drought means something very different for a guy who usually homers every 12 ABs than one who homers every 28. We z-score it: how many standard deviations beyond his typical gap he is right now.
  • The weighting. That z-score is weighted up for proven power (more season HRs = more signal worth trusting) and for confidence (more historical gaps logged = a sturdier personal average, so one early-season fluke doesn't swing things).
  • The contact nudge. Up to ±15% is layered on top based on Statcast's own per-batted-ball contact-quality grade (1-6, weak contact to barrel — already a joint read on exit velo and launch angle), averaged across every batted ball since his last HR vs. before it, and blended with his season rate so one lucky or unlucky swing in a short stretch can't swing the result on its own. Cooling contact nudges his score down even if the raw AB math says he's "due"; contact that's still scalding nudges it up. Exit velo, Sweet Spot%, and Barrel% are shown below for context but don't separately drive the score — they're already folded into that one grade.
  • Eligibility. z ≥ 1.0, 5+ HR this season, AB/HR ≤ 30, and excludes anyone inactive 5+ days (injured, benched, or demoted) — a stale drought doesn't count. A day-to-day guy who's still in the lineup stays fully eligible; his drought is real, he's just playing through something.
  • Who's shown. By default the list hides players who can't bat today — on the IL, team not playing, or out of a posted lineup — since a due hitter who can't play isn't actionable. Their droughts keep tracking in the background; flip off the "Hide Off / Bench / IL" filter to see them. Day-to-day guys (hurt but not IL'd, 🤕, pulled from ESPN's injury report) are handled by lineup: in today's lineup, he plays through it and the tag drops; out of it, he's treated like a benched/injured guy — hidden by default and dropped from the rankings, but tagged 🤕 (not "bench") so the reason is clear. A separate "Hide Day-to-Day" toggle hides them even when the off/bench/IL filter is off.
💤 Off — no game today 🪑 Bench — not in today's starting lineup 🤕 Day-to-Day — hurt, not on the IL 🚑 Injured — on the IL

How Pick Score Works ▶
Today's best HR matchups, combining recent contact quality, platoon edge, pitch-type overlap, pitcher homer rate, and park.

Pick Score = base power × every matchup multiplier below. Base power is each batter's season HR rate (HR/AB) blended toward his Blast%-implied rate and regressed toward league average when the AB sample is thin (4 HR in 51 AB won't hold up a full season, so it's pulled back until it grows). Every factor after that is a × multiplier — the tornado bars in each pick's modal, where 1.00× is neutral, above 1 is juicier, below 1 is tougher.

Every multiplier in the matchup breakdown
  • Recent Contact. Statcast's 1–6 contact-quality grade (weak contact → barrel, a joint read on exit velo + launch angle) over his last ~15 game dates vs his own season baseline — how well he's actually squaring the ball up right now. The same grade the Due tool uses, here time-windowed instead of drought-anchored.
  • Batter Platoon. How this batter's HR rate vs today's pitcher's handedness compares to his rate against the opposite side — his own platoon edge. Shrunk hard toward the league-average platoon split, since one season of HR-rate splits is too thin to trust at face value.
  • Starter Vuln. The flip side: how homer-prone this specific starter is to batters of this hitter's side vs the opposite side. Also shrunk toward league.
  • Arm Stuff. The pitcher's HR-vulnerability from his 4-seam velocity and hard-hit% allowed — both predict homers far better than raw HR/9. Big velo and soft contact pull it below 1× (tougher); fringe velo and loud contact push it above 1× (juicier).
  • Starter Synergy. Overlap between the pitch types this batter has actually homered on this season and the mix the starter throws to this batter's side (not his overall usage — arms attack lefties and righties differently). A fastball-masher facing a pitcher who feeds his side 50% four-seamers scores up; a profile that doesn't match pulls toward neutral. Regressed by HR sample — 3 of 4 homers on fastballs says far less than 30 of 40.
  • Bullpen Vuln. The opponent bullpen's platoon HR-proneness to this batter's side — the rested arms likely to relieve, so the middle innings are priced in too, not just the starter.
  • Bullpen Synergy. The same pitch-type overlap idea, measured against the likely relief mix rather than the starter's.
  • Lineup PA. His batting-order slot → how many plate appearances he projects to get (leadoff ≈ +9%, bottom of the order less). Only applies once a real lineup is known — neutral (and hidden) when the slot is projected.
  • Park. The venue's HR/game rate vs league average — the same park signal shown on the Schedule tab, baked into the score.
  • Weather. Game-time air density (temperature, humidity, pressure, altitude) plus wind projected onto the park's home-plate→center-field axis: hot thin air and a wind blowing out help carry, cold dense air and a wind blowing in suppress it. Roofed parks are neutral.
Chalk vs Value — two lenses on the same slate
  • 🔒 Chalk = proven power. Base power is HR/AB blended toward the Blast%-implied rate, so a hitter squaring the ball up gets credit before the homers land. The standard board keeps bats whose blast-blend power clears a self-calibrating floor (above the league regulars' median, so it means the same in April or August) at Pick Score ≥ 9; flip to Full board to drop the power floor and see every Score-9 candidate. This is your "most likely to go deep" list.
  • 🎯 Value = underpriced power. Ranked by blast surplus — how far a hitter's Blast%-implied HR rate runs ahead of his actual HR total — × matchup. The book prices off HR totals, so a bat squaring it up faster than the homers have landed is underpriced, until they land. A slugger already cashing his blast (surplus ≤ 1) isn't value — he sits on Chalk, so the two boards no longer share names. Value is the long-odds money: only take it at a real price (roughly +600 or better). The pick modal shows both the Value Score and, beneath it, the straight Pick Score so you can see the matchup grade too.
Good to know
  • Lineups. Uses confirmed lineups when posted; before they drop, it falls back to projected starters (15+ games, 1.5+ AB/game, active in the last 7 days, one per position + DH) — the same pool the books use pre-lineup. Projected picks are labeled. Because the Lineup-PA multiplier is neutral pre-lineup, scores tend to firm up (and the Chalk board fill in) once lineups post.
  • Openers. When today's "starter" is really an opener (2+ starts averaging ≤3 IP on a team using the piggyback pattern), his weight drops to the inning or two he covers and the likely bulk arm gets his own platoon + pitch-mix slice of the score. Flagged in the pick modal so you know who you're really betting against.

How Day Late Works ▶
Yesterday's Chalk picks that didn't go yard — bounce-back candidates for today.
  • The premise. HR luck is day-to-day, so a strong pick that missed is worth another look. Shows yesterday's proven-power Picks that didn't homer, with the rank they carried.
  • No re-scoring. On purpose — the whole idea is that yesterday's miss doesn't change today's odds. Filters let you hide anyone off/benched/injured, drop day-to-day guys, or limit to bats also on today's board.

Returning Boppers ▶
Injured power bats and when they're due back — the first game off the injury is a classic homer spot.
  • Who's here. Power bats on an injured list or day-to-day per ESPN's injury report — anyone with at least one homer this season, so pitchers and no-power scrubs are filtered out. Each shows his season homers and today's Due / Pick badges where they apply. Tracked returns (the Results tab) are held to a higher bar — 5+ HR — so the return-day homer rate reflects real boppers.
  • Return dates. ESPN's estimated return date — a target, not a promise. Near-term dates are usually solid; a date weeks out can slide with setbacks or rehab stints.
  • Just back. When a hitter drops off the injury report (and isn't on the IL), he's flagged here for his first 3 days back — the "homers in his first game back" window. A guy still on the report but confirmed in today's lineup counts as back too.
  • Results. Every flagged return is tracked: homer within that 3-day window and he moves to the Results tab as a hit; the window closes with no homer and he's logged as a miss. The tab shows the running return-day homer rate.

How the Steal Board Works ▶
Today's base-stealers ranked against the battery they'll actually face — runner speed & tendency vs pitcher hand and catcher arm.
  • The model. Backtested on this season's steals, it's two stages: who runs is driven by the runner (speed, lead, his own attempt rate) and the pitcher's handedness (lefties hold runners ~26% better); whether it works is driven by the catcher (pop time + arm → caught-stealing rate), not raw runner speed or pitcher tempo.
  • Run%. How often he takes off per time on base, from his own season attempt rate, adjusted for the pitcher's hand.
  • Safe%. His success rate vs this catcher — his steal-success blended with the catcher's caught-stealing rate (a runner × catcher log5).
  • Steal. The headline rank — expected successful steals per 100 times on base (Run% × Safe%).

How Pitch Types Works ▶
How every team — and every hitter — performs against each pitch type this season.
  • Pick a pitch up top, then the 30 teams rank by how they hit it. Tap a team to drill into its individual hitters vs that pitch.
  • BA & SLG are the headline — team numbers pool every batter's at-bats against that pitch (volume-weighted). Sort by any column.
  • Barrel% · Sweet-Spot% · Hard-Hit% come from Statcast batted balls split by pitch — the quality-of-contact read behind the results. Whiff% is swing-and-miss (lower is better for the hitter).
  • Contact (1–6) is the average of Statcast's per-batted-ball grade — every ball in play scored 1 = weak · 2 = topped · 3 = under · 4 = flare/burner · 5 = solid · 6 = barrel (its joint read of exit velo + launch angle). Higher = harder, more homer-shaped contact; ~2.5 is roughly league-average.
  • Batters need 10+ pitches of a type to qualify (low so rare pitches like splitters still get team-wide coverage; team numbers are PA-weighted, so small samples barely move them). Data is 2026 regular season, via Baseball Savant.
What each category includes
  • 4-Seam Fastball, Sinker (two-seamers fold in here), Cutter, Changeup — as named.
  • Slider — sliders + slurves.
  • Sweeper — its own category, not lumped into Slider (the big modern split).
  • Curveball — curveballs + knuckle curves + slow curves.
  • Split-Finger — splitters + forkballs.
  • Eephus, knuckleball, and unclassified pitches are left out. Everything's grouped the way Baseball Savant groups it.

How Milestones Work ▶
Hitters closing in on a round-hundred career HR total — 100, 200, 300 and up.
  • Closing In. Flags anyone within 3 homers of their next round hundred — career HR milestones only, no 50s — ranked by how close, with a pace estimate from this year's rate.
  • Reached. Collects everyone who's crossed a hundred this season.
  • No ceiling. Tracking isn't capped at 300 — a 400th, 500th, or 600th chase shows up the same way.
